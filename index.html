Robots I have worked on

Dual-arm robot at a London trade show

Robotic Research Engineer (part-time) · 2026

  • Motions taught in my own tool ran on the team’s physical dual-arm robot. It loaded parcels live for two days at the Parcel+Post Expo, ExCeL London (September 2026).
  • Built a no-code teaching tool in Python and ROS 2: create dual-arm motions step by step, replay them in simulation, and check every pose against the real robot’s joint limits.
  • Solved the arm’s inverse kinematics and moved the hand in straight lines, so parcels went in without clipping the box.

The motions were pre-programmed, not autonomous or vision-driven. The team built the hardware; I wrote the motion software.

Wearable robotic tail (PhD)

University of Reading · 2020 to 2026

  • Designed and built a wearable robotic tail triggered by the wearer’s own arm muscle (EMG): embedded C++ on one main Arduino and three secondary boards (I2C), with stepper drivers.
  • Built the test rig and a MATLAB/Simulink pipeline that recorded brain signals (EEG), muscle signals (EMG) and foot pressure together.
  • The tail ran reliably through a 25-person study.

Award: Eurohaptics 2024, Best Work In Progress, Honourable Mention.

Open-source project

ROS 2 OpenArm Pick-and-Place

A ROS 2 (Jazzy) simulation of a 7-DOF OpenArm manipulator that picks parcels off a table and inserts them into a box, then extends to two arms working separately or together.

Simulation only. Grasping uses a kinematic “attach to gripper frame” step, not contact physics.

  • Inverse kinematics on a 7-DOF arm (roboticstoolbox-python, Levenberg–Marquardt), checked against forward kinematics.
  • Straight-line hand motion for box insertion (resolved-rate control with damped least-squares near singularities).
  • Two arms from a programmatically composed URDF: a shared workspace where only one arm enters at a time, and a two-handed carry.
  • Node pipeline: target publisher → IK planner → joint-state bridge → robot_state_publisher → TF → rviz2.
